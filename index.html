Everloom imagines an AI agent's memory that never ends. Each fact is captured with its source, checked at an identity gate, kept on the owner's device and shared only through scoped, audited threads. Pick a stage to fly in.

Concept · design intent, not a production certification

A memory with a constitution

Continuity, with boundaries.

Everloom is designed around consent, provenance and an owner's final say. These are design principles. Their enforcement varies by implementation stage.

01

Capture with context

Record a fact alongside its source, scope, time and lifecycle. Keep uncertainty visible instead of turning guesses into history.

02

Share only when allowed

Agents should receive the smallest useful context, only under verified identity and explicit permissions.

03

Correct, revoke, restore

Surface conflicting edits, review an audit trail and recover from known-good backups rather than silently replacing truth.

    For builders

    An interface that earns trust.

    Everloom's proposed agent interfaces keep identity outside of user-controlled payloads. Requests are authorized server-side; each grant scopes both owner and memory classification. The examples here are concepts, not a public API.

    Questions worth asking

    Clarity is a feature.

    Can I store passwords or sensitive documents in Everloom today?

    No. The project remains a prototype. Do not entrust it with actual secrets or identity papers before a tested, approved secure release.

    Does the site collect or synchronize my demo memories?

    No. Its sample records and anything you type live only in JavaScript memory in this browser tab. Refreshing the page clears them.

    Does Everloom make an AI permanently conscious or continuously connected?

    No. It is software for bounded memory records and task handoff, not an always-on consciousness or a magical connection between providers.

    Where does my data ultimately belong?

    The design goal is to keep the owner in control of storage, permissions, export and deletion. Complete production guarantees still require testing.
